## Idea

Kiko is a small companion device with a face. The goal is that it feels like a companion and not like a gadget: it sees the person it belongs to, recognises them, and reacts to them.

Everything it does starts with what the camera sees, so the vision has to work before anything else can.

## My part

The computer vision, written in Python. The first task is recognising the owner from camera images, in a normal room with normal light, fast enough to react in real time.

## Current status

An early prototype on the bench. The face display, a camera and the compute board run together. The vision work is in progress, which is why the screen in the clip says the vision models are not installed.

There are no results to report yet, so this page has none.

## What I will measure

Once the first model runs on the device, the numbers that matter are how often it recognises the right person, how often it is wrong about a stranger, and how many frames per second the board can handle. They will be published here, including the weak ones.

## What I want to learn

Computer vision on real hardware is new for me. Kiko is how I am learning what changes when a model has to work on a real device, in a real room, in real time.
